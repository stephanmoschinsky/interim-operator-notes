# Interim Weekly Operating Review

## Purpose of the Weekly Review
The weekly operating review exists to maintain clarity under uncertainty.

It is not a reporting ritual.
It is not a performance review.
It is a decision-support mechanism.

Its purpose is to surface what matters
before momentum turns into misalignment.

## What the Review Is Not
The weekly review is explicitly not used to:

- showcase activity
- justify past decisions
- optimise metrics
- resolve political tensions

These dynamics are addressed elsewhere or not at all.

## Core Questions Reviewed Every Week
Every weekly review revolves around a small set of questions:

- What did we learn this week that changes our understanding?
- Which assumptions were reinforced or invalidated?
- Where did decisions stall, and why?
- What became clearer — and what became noisier?

If these questions cannot be answered,
the review has failed.

## Decision Inventory
Each review includes a short inventory of decisions:

- Decisions taken
- Decisions delayed
- Decisions avoided

Avoided decisions are explicitly named,
as they often carry the highest cost.

## Risk and Irreversibility Check
The review explicitly checks for emerging irreversibility:

- New fixed costs
- Implicit commitments made to customers or partners
- Structural assumptions becoming normalised

Anything that reduces optionality
is surfaced early.

## Market Signal Quality
Market signals are reviewed for quality, not volume.

- Direct vs. interpreted feedback
- Consistency across conversations
- Signals that contradict internal narratives

Increased activity without improved signal quality
is treated as a warning sign.

## Organisational Load
The review assesses organisational load:

- Where effort compensates for missing structure
- Where individuals absorb ambiguity without mandate
- Where escalation replaces ownership

Rising load without increased clarity
indicates structural stress.

## Outcomes of the Review
A weekly review produces only a limited set of outcomes:

- Decisions to be taken
- Assumptions to be tested
- Activities to be stopped

Anything beyond this dilutes focus.

## When the Review Loses Its Value
The weekly review loses its value when:

- It turns into a reporting exercise
- Questions are answered with narratives
- Decisions are postponed without consequence
- The same issues reappear without escalation

At this point, the issue is not execution,
but ownership.
