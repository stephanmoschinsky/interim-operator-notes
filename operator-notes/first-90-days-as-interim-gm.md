# The First 90 Days as Interim General Manager

## Purpose of the First 90 Days
The first 90 days are not about acceleration.

They exist to convert assumptions into operational reality
before irreversible commitments are made.

The purpose is clarity:
- what works
- what does not
- and what cannot be fixed through effort alone

## What Must Be Clear on Day 1
On day one, only a few things must be clear:

- Who owns the mandate and decision authority
- Which decisions cannot be delegated
- Where escalation ends and responsibility begins
- What success would make the role obsolete

Anything beyond this creates false certainty.

## What Is Explicitly Not Optimised Early
Several areas are intentionally not optimised in the early phase:

- Efficiency
- Team structure
- Performance metrics
- Process documentation

Optimising too early stabilises assumptions
that have not yet been validated.

## Where Time Is Actually Spent
Contrary to expectation, most time is not spent on execution.

Time is absorbed by:
- resolving ownership conflicts
- clarifying decision paths
- correcting misaligned expectations
- reducing noise created by parallel initiatives

These activities are rarely visible,
but they determine whether execution becomes possible.

## Signals to Watch Closely
Certain signals matter more than formal reporting:

- Speed of decision-making under uncertainty
- Quality of first customer conversations
- Willingness to stop initiatives without justification theatre
- Degree to which responsibility is accepted rather than discussed

These signals appear early
and are difficult to fake.

## What Must Be True After 90 Days
After 90 days, several things must be true:

- The organisation can make informed go / no-go decisions
- Ownership for next-phase execution is unambiguous
- Market feedback is direct rather than interpreted
- Remaining risks are explicit, not hidden

If these conditions are met,
the mandate has served its purpose.

## When the Role Is Not Working
The role is not working when:

- Decisions remain political rather than operational
- Progress is measured through activity rather than clarity
- Responsibility is continuously renegotiated
- The organisation prefers comfort over correction

In such cases, extending the mandate increases cost
without increasing insight.
