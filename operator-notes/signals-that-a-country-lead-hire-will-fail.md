# Signals That a Country Lead Hire Will Fail

## Context
Hiring a country lead is often treated as a milestone
in market entry.

It signals commitment.
It creates a sense of progress.
It appears to transfer responsibility.

In practice, the hire often absorbs uncertainty
that should have been resolved structurally.

## This Is Not About Capability
Most failing country lead hires are not caused by lack of competence.

They fail because the environment does not allow the role
to function as intended.

The signals below describe structural conditions,
not individual shortcomings.

## Signal 1: The Role Exists to Create Momentum
When the primary purpose of the role
is to demonstrate progress rather than to carry ownership,
the hire is exposed.

Momentum is a poor substitute for mandate clarity.

## Signal 2: Decision Authority Is Implicit
If decision authority is assumed rather than explicitly defined,
the role becomes reactive.

Approval replaces judgment.
Escalation replaces ownership.

## Signal 3: Success Is Defined as Activity
When success is measured through activity
rather than through learning or decision clarity,
the role is incentivised to produce motion.

This masks unresolved questions
and delays necessary corrections.

## Signal 4: The Hire Is Used to Absorb Uncertainty
Country leads are often hired to reduce discomfort at headquarters.

Uncertainty is transferred into a role
without providing the authority required to resolve it.

The role becomes a buffer, not an owner.

## Signal 5: Local Presence Is Expected to Fix Structural Gaps
Physical presence does not compensate for missing structure.

When a country lead is expected to resolve
unclear strategy, fragmented ownership, or decision latency,
the role is set up to fail.

## Signal 6: Exit Conditions Are Undefined
If it is unclear under which conditions the role would be re-scoped,
paused, or exited,
failure becomes a personal outcome rather than a structural signal.

This increases defensiveness
and reduces transparency.

## Why These Signals Matter
Country lead failures rarely fail loudly.

They erode trust.
They accumulate friction.
They delay learning.

By the time replacement is discussed,
the underlying conditions often remain unchanged.

## What Works Better Instead
Before hiring a country lead,
several things must be true:

- Decision authority is explicit and limited
- Success is defined through learning and clarity
- Structural uncertainty is acknowledged, not hidden
- Exit conditions are agreed upfront

Hiring then becomes a transfer of responsibility,
not a delegation of discomfort.
