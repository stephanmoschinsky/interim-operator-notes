# Organisational Reset Memo

## Context
The organisation had grown over time through incremental decisions
rather than deliberate organisational design.

Roles had evolved organically.
Decision paths reflected historical compromises.
Accountability was distributed across functions and layers.

While the organisation continued to operate,
friction increased and execution quality declined.
Symptoms were visible, but causes remained diffuse.

## Decision at Hand
The decision was whether to continue operating within the existing structure
or to initiate a deliberate organisational reset.

This required choosing between short-term continuity
and the disruption necessary to restore clarity of ownership and execution.

## Irreversible Commitments
Maintaining the existing structure would have reinforced several irreversible dynamics:

- Normalisation of unclear ownership
- Further role accumulation without mandate clarity
- Decision-making through escalation rather than responsibility
- Increasing dependency on individual heroics

Over time, these patterns would have become structural
rather than situational.

## Options Considered
Three options were considered:

1. Maintain the current structure and address issues incrementally
2. Introduce targeted adjustments without changing core design
3. Reset the organisational structure to re-establish clear ownership

## Decision
The decision was to initiate an organisational reset
focused on restoring clear ownership, decision authority, and execution paths.

## Rationale
Incremental fixes would not have addressed the underlying problem.

The organisation did not lack talent or effort.
It lacked a structure that made responsibility explicit.

Without resetting roles and decision paths,
execution would have continued to rely on informal workarounds
rather than accountable ownership.

## Consequences
The organisational reset carried immediate consequences:

- Temporary disruption of established routines
- Loss of comfort for individuals benefiting from ambiguity
- Short-term slowdown as roles and responsibilities were clarified

In return, it created the conditions
for sustainable execution,
clear accountability,
and reduced dependency on escalation.

## What Would Have Broken Otherwise
Without a reset, ambiguity would have continued to compound.

Decision-making would have slowed further.
Execution gaps would have been addressed through effort rather than structure.
Key individuals would have absorbed increasing load without mandate clarity.

Over time, the organisation would have become resilient to change,
but fragile in execution.

The reset prevented structural ambiguity
from becoming the organisation’s default operating mode.
